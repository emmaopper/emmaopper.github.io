Kinetic-Informed Data-Driven Modeling for the SAIR
======
The Space–Atmosphere Interaction Region (SAIR), which sits in very Low Earth Orbit (vLEO), is a little-understood layer of the upper atmosphere where future satellites may operate. Flying at these altitudes could enable higher-resolution Earth observations, faster communications, and lower mission costs, but predicting the environment around a spacecraft becomes significantly more challenging. In this region, the assumptions underlying the fluid equations used in most atmospheric and aerospace models begin to break down. As a result, important processes that influence satellite drag, species transport, and energy deposition are often poorly represented in existing models.

My research seeks to bridge this gap between traditional fluid models and computationally expensive kinetic simulations. Using high-fidelity particle-based simulations as a reference, I am developing machine-learning-enhanced continuum models that retain the efficiency of classical fluid solvers while capturing the non-equilibrium physics that emerge in rarefied flows. The goal is to improve our ability to model the upper atmosphere and provide more accurate environmental predictions for both whole-atmosphere simulations and future spacecraft operating in vLEO.

Ultimately, the goal is to develop a 3D operational model that can be incorporated into existing whole-atmosphere simulations. As a first step, I am investigating these ideas in a 1D toy problem, where the physics can be isolated and the learned closures can be rigorously validated. I presented early results from this work at the SIAM Conference on Mathematics of Planet Earth (MPE) in July 2026.
